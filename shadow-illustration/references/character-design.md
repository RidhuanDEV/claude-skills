# Character and mascot construction

Build a small model sheet from the approved master. Record head and torso relationship, shoulder width, eye line, hair masses, eyewear, clothing landmarks, hand size, line weight and shadow direction. Add front, three-quarter or side views only when needed. Separate identity landmarks from allowed pose and expression variables.

1. Block the pose with a line of action and dominant silhouette. Mark shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips and gaze before contours. Place joints with believable spacing for the view and account for foreshortening.
2. Construct each limb as a continuous volume. A sleeve should overlap the upper arm. Give the elbow a readable turn, taper toward the wrist, and carry the wrist into the palm. Match palm mass against the master at the same scale. An open hand will occupy a different bounding box than a fist.
3. Block palm direction and thumb origin before adding fingers. Use overlap to communicate depth. Compare the resulting gesture without an icon or caption. Draw an alternative if the motion reads as a wave when it should be presenting or shrugging.
4. For motion, place shoulder, elbow and wrist pivots where their shapes meet. Inspect silhouettes at the extremes and intermediate pose. Use overlapping geometry or controlled deformation to avoid visible seams.
5. Compare the master and all rendered variants side by side. Review clothing, glasses, and hair as carefully as the face. Correct the affected limb, expression, or crop before duplicating the pose across states.

Use readable gestures rather than one pose for every state. For a mascot system, retain the master, model notes and approved variants. Without renders, record character consistency as unverified.
