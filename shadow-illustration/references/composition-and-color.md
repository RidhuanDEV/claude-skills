# Composition and color

Start with the screen slot, aspect ratio, supported crop modes, copy region and smallest display size. Draw or annotate safe areas. Keep the face, hand and status symbol clear of clipping, overlays and corners. Use thumbnail and grayscale views to check silhouette and value grouping.

- Give each scene one dominant focal point. Place contextual shapes so gaze and gesture support the point the user should notice. Keep surrounding detail visibly subordinate.
- Balance visual mass, including apparent stroke weight and fill. Compare motifs at the same scale rather than matching path bounds. Use negative space to separate a hand, prop, face and text region.
- Sample color from established tokens or an approved palette. Assign colors to surface, outline, light, shadow and semantic roles. Use tone and silhouette alongside hue for semantic meaning.
- Check visual consistency on supported light and dark surfaces. Do not assume an opaque background or alpha is desired. Keep application text separate and verify its contrast in the real UI.
- For isometric positioning, align repeated edges to consistent axes. For perspective, keep horizon, overlap and scale coherent within a scene.

Compare placement at the narrowest and widest intended slots, with the real headline and CTA when available. Check focus, clipping, and whether the scene still communicates at that size. Record the rendered crop used to make the judgment.
