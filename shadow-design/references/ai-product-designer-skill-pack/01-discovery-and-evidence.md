# Discovery and evidence hierarchy
## Minimum discovery contract
Document target users/roles, user objective and JTBD (`When [context], I want [motivation], so I can [outcome]`), frequency, motivation, constraints, environment/device, existing alternatives, organization outcome, and non-goals. Stakeholder request is not automatically evidence of user demand.

## Evidence strength
Observed user behavior/analytics > directly gathered qualitative research > support tickets and workflow logs > stakeholder assertion > designer/AI assumption. Strength depends on sampling, freshness, and study quality. Use `EVIDENCE` with origin/date; `ASSUMPTION` with risk and test; `PROPOSAL` with rationale. Never invent sources or user personas.

## Methods
User interviews: ask for recent real behavior, not hypothetical preferences. Contextual inquiry for operational workflows. Journey mapping for cross-channel tasks. Card sorting for IA labeling; tree testing for findability. Opportunity-solution trees and assumption mapping for prioritization. Use small, testable hypotheses.

## Requirement triage
Distinguish must-have job completion, compliance/security needs, nice-to-have, out-of-scope, and unknown. Record functional and nonfunctional constraints. Avoid optimizing business KPI via deceptive UX.

## Output
Problem statement; personas as hypotheses if unresearched; success measures; 5 highest-risk assumptions; research/validation plan; traceable story IDs. If research is unavailable, proceed carefully and visibly flag validation needs.
